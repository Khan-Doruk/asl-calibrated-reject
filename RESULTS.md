## Stage 1 — split protocol, everything else held fixed

Same features, same BiGRU, same 5 seeds, same temperature procedure, one shared
pool of 1120 clips over 100 glosses. Only the split assignment varies.

Condition A reproduced the committed baseline **bit-identically**, seed for
seed, which is what makes the three columns comparable.

| | split | train | test | signers shared train↔test | top-1 | ECE before | ECE after | T* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | signer-disjoint (committed baseline) | 616 | 224 | 0 | 0.232 ± 0.024 | 0.247 ± 0.031 | 0.079 ± 0.010 | 1.58 ± 0.10 |
| **B** | WLASL standard split, cal carved from train | 638 | 128 | 36 | 0.383 ± 0.023 | 0.231 ± 0.036 | 0.126 ± 0.013 | 1.85 ± 0.15 |
| **C** | standard split, train subsampled to 616 to match A | 616 | 128 | 36 | 0.342 ± 0.019 | 0.270 ± 0.041 | 0.106 ± 0.028 | 1.93 ± 0.16 |

### What it says

**The protocol gap is +0.110** — 0.232 (A) → 0.342 (C) with training volume
held equal at 616 vs 616 clips. Moving from a signer-disjoint split to WLASL's
standard split, changing nothing else, buys about 11 points.

**B vs C is not a real difference.** They differ by 22 training clips and
+0.041 accuracy, which is ~5 clips on a 128-clip test set, against seed
spreads of ±0.023 and ±0.019. Treat B and C as replicates. The standard split
in this pool holds 806 train clips, so after carving a calibration set it
offers almost no volume advantage over A — the confound C was built to control
is nearly absent.

**Calibration behaves the same under both protocols.** Every condition is
overconfident before scaling (0.247, 0.231, 0.270) and improves after. Residual ECE
looks worse on the standard split (0.079 vs 0.106), but the bootstrap
intervals overlap — A [0.061, 0.139] against C [0.082, 0.199] — and C's test
set is only 128 clips. **Do not claim calibration transfers worse on the standard
split.** It is not supported.

### The confound, and why it cannot be removed here

A and C use different test sets (224 vs 128 clips, 98 vs 85 glosses), because each
protocol defines its own. So +0.110 mixes signer overlap with test-set
difficulty, and is an **upper bound** on the signer-overlap effect, not a
measurement of it.

Two designs would isolate it, and neither is possible on this pool:

- *Train on signers disjoint from the standard test set.* The standard test set
  draws on 39 of the pool's signers, leaving only
  149 clips from other signers — against the
  784 needed.
- *Split the standard test set by whether its signer was trained on.* Of its
  128 clips, **125 come from a signer the model trained on and only
  3 do not.**

That second number is the mechanism, measured directly:
**98% of the standard test set is signers the model has already seen.**
That is the clearest statement of why the standard protocol flatters a model,
and it is worth more to the write-up than the +0.110 itself.

**Our pool is not the official WLASL100.** It was built as the 100
most-represented glosses *on the HuggingFace mirror*, which hosts part of WLASL.
Against the official benchmark: **58/100 glosses in common**, and we hold
678 of its 2038 videos. The official split is
1442 train / 338 val / 258 test — that ~1400 is where the plan's
figure came from, but it is not what this pool contains.

So **no condition here is comparable to Pose-GRU (46.51) or ST-GCN (50.78)**,
on either protocol. Even rebuilding the official gloss list would not fix it:
only 1007 of its 2038 videos (49%) are on the mirror at all.
